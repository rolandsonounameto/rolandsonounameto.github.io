About

I am a biostatistician and malaria modeller based in Cotonou, Benin. My PhD in Biometry, at the University of Abomey-Calavi with the African Institute for Mathematical Sciences (AIMS-RIC) and the Swiss Tropical and Public Health Institute (Swiss TPH), assesses the impact of malaria interventions in Benin through mathematical modelling.

I am a Bill & Melinda Gates Scholar in the MaModAfrica programme, supervised by Prof. Romain Glèlè Kakaï and Dr Emilie Pothin. Before the PhD I spent several years as a survey manager and data analyst on evaluations funded by the World Bank, GIZ, the Hewlett Foundation and others. That fieldwork shapes how I work with routine data today.

Interests

  • Infectious disease epidemiology and statistical modelling: impact evaluation of malaria interventions, spatial and spatio-temporal analysis, and mathematical and agent-based modelling.
  • Cost-effectiveness analysis: budget-constrained optimisation to prioritise and allocate malaria control interventions.
  • Research-to-policy translation: turning research findings into clear, actionable evidence for national programmes and policymakers.

Research experience

    PhD Fellow, Biometry

    Evaluated Seasonal Malaria Chemoprevention (SMC) in northern Benin (2019–2022) with a synthetic control design and negative binomial mixed-effects models; published in BMJ Public Health. Calibrated a Weibull decay function for SMC protection across eight trials in OpenMalaria. Calibrated OpenMalaria to Benin's health districts and am building a budget-constrained framework that ranks intervention packages by cost-effectiveness.
